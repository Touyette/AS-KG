The four-stage learning model, first described by Broadwell in 1969, is mapped onto attachment work: running the pattern without seeing it, seeing it with none of the equipment to change it, doing otherwise with deliberate effort every time, and eventually doing otherwise by default. The second stage is rated the most frustrating, because awareness arrives long before capacity and the gap gets read as personal defect rather than sequence. Symptoms that look unrelated — procrastination, picked fights, eating without hunger — turn out to share one root, and a failed attempt counts as information about conditions.
