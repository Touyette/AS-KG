Roughly half to sixty per cent of people are secure, anxious and avoidant sit near twenty per cent each, and fearful-avoidant is rarest by a wide margin, which is why it collects people who can see both patterns in themselves but cannot yet tell them apart. The same behaviours run across styles and mean opposite things: storming out or threatening to end it belongs to both repertoires. Each insecure style travels toward something it never arrives at, while a settled nervous system can afford to look squarely at what a parent got wrong.
