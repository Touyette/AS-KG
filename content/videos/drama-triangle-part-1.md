Karpman's triangle, devised in the late 1960s out of transactional analysis, describes three roles people rotate through in a dispute. The victim enters as the one things happen to, and therefore not the one who can fix them; the persecutor holds the same conviction of having been wronged but delivers it as a demand; the saviour sets its own needs aside, on the premise that being needed is how love is earned, and so quietly requires the other person not to recover. The test is the sentence I cannot be okay until they change.
