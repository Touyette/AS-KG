Guilt says a mistake was made; shame says one is the mistake. The timing accounts for the difference: shame is available from around fifteen months, before a child knows itself to be separate from its caregiver, so an unreturned bid registers not as rejection but as the self coming apart. Guilt arrives later and depends on mentalizing, on holding that another mind runs on different contents than one's own. Shame produces private misery and buried episodes rather than amends, and the test is which way the feeling pulls.
