Distress goes on a scale of one to ten, and the organising question is where a caregiver reliably stepped in. Attuned care supplies two things, protection and comfort, and receiving one without the other produces the two insecure conclusions: that bad feeling is itself the problem and cannot be headed off alone, or that behaving correctly prevents rejection while pain, once it lands, is faced without company. At moderate distress the tells diverge accordingly, into preoccupation with whether someone is annoyed, or frustration at a body that will not perform to specification.
