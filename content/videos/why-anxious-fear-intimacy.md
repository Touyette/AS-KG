The anxious version of fearing intimacy looks like its opposite: needing every conversation to reach the core immediately, finding anything lighter unbearable, and reading a limit as a wall rather than as the thing that makes two people visible to each other. Noticing where one person ends and another begins is the precondition for intimacy, not an obstacle to it; what replaces it is enmeshment. Underneath sits a harder layer, the position of innocence, and the line between two people who can each hold themselves and choose to lean, and two who cannot.
