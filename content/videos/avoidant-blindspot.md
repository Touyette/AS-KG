The avoidant wheel is over-inflated on the analytic side: understanding why something happened mattered more, early on, than processing how it felt, and the skill became excellent. The usual stereotype gets corrected here, since the pain is not being concealed from other people so much as withheld from the person having it. What follows is a conviction of being able to bear anything and, which Priebe names as the largest obstacle in her own healing, a settled belief that telling anyone what hurts serves no purpose.
