## Does this sound like you?

You would say you want a long-term relationship and mean it. What tends to happen is that someone you were genuinely interested in stops landing, without an event that explains it — and it arrives feeling like clarity, like you have finally seen them properly.

You solve your own problems. You are good in a crisis. You may not be able to locate what you feel until it is large, and when you do find something that does not seem warranted, your instinct is to work out why it is mistaken. Other people's visible need can produce something closer to disgust than sympathy.

None of that is a character defect. It is what a system learns when reaching for someone reliably cost more than it returned.

> [!tip] You can be different in different relationships
> These are descriptions of what a system does under stress, not personality types. People run differently with different people and at different times, and the point of reading one is recognition, not classification.

## When you're in it

- [**I messed up and I don't know what to do now**](/toolbox/#after-i-messed-up) — For the hours after you blew up, went cold, or said the thing — when shame is loudest and the urge is to fix it immediately.
- [**I'm angry and I can't tell if it's proportionate**](/toolbox/#anger-out-of-proportion) — For anger that feels either far too big for what happened, or suspiciously absent.
- [**I feel nothing and I think I should**](/toolbox/#feeling-nothing) — For the gap between what a situation warrants and what you can actually locate in yourself.
- [**I've gone cold on someone and I don't know why**](/toolbox/#gone-cold) — For when someone you were genuinely into stopped landing, with no event that explains it.
- [**We keep having the same fight**](/toolbox/#same-fight-again) — For the argument that resolves and then returns, in a slightly different costume.
- [**They say I hurt them and I don't see it**](/toolbox/#they-say-i-hurt-them) — For being told you did something you did not intend and cannot yet recognise — before the defence writes itself.
- [**I'm thinking about getting back together with them**](/toolbox/#thinking-about-going-back) — For the pull toward a relationship that already ended once — before you act on it rather than after.
- [**There's something I want to say and it feels too small**](/toolbox/#too-small-to-mention) — For the thing you keep deciding not to raise, that keeps not going away.

## Core strategies

- [[strategies/affect-suppression|Affect Suppression]] — The system does not merely hide hurt from other people — it withholds the experience from the person having it.
- [[strategies/deactivating-strategies|Deactivating Strategies]] — The avoidant threat response. Once the body registers that it has grown dependent on someone, the system stops running the program that noticed what it liked about them and starts running one that scans for cost.
- [[strategies/victim-position|Victim Position]] — Entering conflict as the one things happen to: I did not cause this, so I cannot and should not be the one to fix it.
- [[strategies/resource-threat-scanning|Resource Threat Scanning]] — What the avoidant system starts doing once attachment comes online: auditing the relationship for what it will cost.
- [[strategies/the-social-mask|The Social Mask]] — Most fearful-avoidant people spend more time in one response than the other, and build an identity on it — the analytical one, or the selfless and giving one — while carrying quiet embarrassment about the other.
- [[strategies/radical-self-responsibility|Radical Self-Responsibility]] — Every problem, and particularly every emotional one, treated as a thing to be worked out alone.
- [[strategies/rationalizing-the-feeling-away|Rationalizing The Feeling Away]] — The adult mechanism: scan the environment for a justification, and if none is blatant enough, conclude the feeling itself is mistaken.
- [[strategies/savior-position|Saviour Position]] — Seeking out people who seem to need fixing and setting your own needs aside for them.
- [[strategies/persecutor-position|Persecutor Position]] — The same conviction of having been wronged, delivered forcefully rather than plaintively: here is what you did, here is what you must change.
- [[strategies/preemptive-sabotage|Preemptive Sabotage]] — If something is certain to go wrong, going first is the only move that returns any control.
- [[strategies/sequence-analysis|Sequence Analysis]] — The avoidant over-inflated wheel: learning early that understanding why something happened mattered more than processing how it felt, and getting very good at it.
- [[strategies/environmental-control|Environmental Control]] — A whole life quietly arranged so that mood stays predictable without anyone else's involvement.

## The ideas underneath

- [[concepts/attunement|Attunement]] — Reading another person's state, letting your own respond to it, and reflecting it back accurately before helping settle it.
- [[concepts/complex-causality|Complex Causality]] — The security position, and the exit from the blame see-saw: most conflicts are produced by two people acting in good faith whose triggers and unexamined programmes collide.
- [[concepts/interdependence-versus-codependence|Interdependence Versus Codependence]] — The line she draws at the end: two people who can each hold themselves and choose to lean on one another are interdependent.
- [[concepts/adaptive-versus-healthy|Adaptive Versus Healthy]] — Something can be exactly right for the situation that produced it and ruinous over a lifetime.
- [[concepts/knowing-the-mechanism|Knowing The Mechanism]] — Her answer to why understanding the origin matters at all, given that understanding changes nothing by itself.
- [[concepts/not-your-fault-but-your-responsibility|Not Your Fault, But Your Responsibility]] — The principle she puts at the centre of the whole endeavour, and the one most often split in half.
- [[concepts/conscious-competence-model|The Conscious Competence Model]] — The four-stage frame she maps healing onto: running the pattern without seeing it, running it while seeing it, being able to do otherwise with deliberate effort, and finally doing otherwise without effort.
- [[concepts/co-regulation|Co-Regulation]] — Two nervous systems settling each other. Mostly mundane — getting used to someone replying, to their presence in a week — rather than the dramatic comforting the word suggests, which is why becoming…
- [[concepts/differentiation|Differentiation]] — Knowing where you end and another person begins — which feeling in the room is yours.
- [[concepts/intimacy-requires-boundaries|Intimacy Requires Boundaries]] — Her central reframe here: noticing where you end and someone else begins is not an obstacle to intimacy, it is the precondition for it.
- [[concepts/meta-feelings-about-the-oscillation|Meta-Feelings About The Oscillation]] — Fearful-avoidance is not only anxious triggers plus avoidant triggers.
- [[concepts/attachment-is-not-a-personality-type|Not A Personality Type]] — Why the usual personality-system habits do not transfer.

## Core beliefs

- [[beliefs/i-am-not-okay-and-neither-are-you|I Am Not Okay And Neither Are You]] — The fearful-avoidant position, and the exact inverse of the secure one.
- [[beliefs/vulnerability-leads-to-rejection|Vulnerability Leads To Rejection]] — Learned where reaching out was reliably met with withdrawal or hostility.
- [[beliefs/everything-that-happens-is-my-doing|Everything That Happens Is My Doing]] — Every child starts life assuming they are the cause of what happens around them.
- [[beliefs/i-cannot-be-okay-until-they-change|I Cannot Be Okay Until They Change]] — Her single test for whether you are in the triangle.
- [[beliefs/something-is-wrong-with-me|Something Is Wrong With Me]] — The deepest layer, and she says every insecure style holds some version of it.
- [[beliefs/depending-on-others-costs-me-myself|Depending On Others Costs Me Myself]] — The premise underneath the cost audit: that what a relationship asks for comes out of the same finite supply that keeps you regulated.
- [[beliefs/my-partner-should-never-be-dysregulated|My Partner Should Never Be Dysregulated]] — The avoidant version of an impossible expectation, and the mirror of the anxious one.
- [[beliefs/relationships-are-net-cost|Relationships Only Take]] — The conclusion that follows from never bringing your own wants to a relationship: if you do not ask it for anything, it cannot give you anything, and the ledger will always read as loss.
- [[beliefs/showing-emotion-does-not-work|Showing Emotion Does Not Get Needs Met]] — The conclusion drawn from repeated refusal.
- [[beliefs/i-can-protect-myself-but-not-comfort-myself|I Can Protect Myself But Not Comfort Myself]] — The avoidant inverse. Behaving correctly heads off rejection, so protection is learnable — but if pain lands anyway you are alone with it.
- [[beliefs/i-lost-myself-in-that-relationship|I Lost Myself In That Relationship]] — The central error she names, and the one that guarantees repetition.
- [[beliefs/pursue-but-never-relax|Pursue But Never Relax]] — The instruction a child derives when the same person is both the source of care and the source of threat: you must pursue closeness because you need it to be alright, and you must never settle into it.

## How it shows up

- [[behaviors/looking-down-on-the-partner|Looking Down On The Partner]] — Contempt made operational. Writing the other person off as incapable removes the need to be vulnerable with them — and removes their chance to do better, because they are no longer being handed anything real to meet.
- [[behaviors/minimizing-what-you-are-going-through|Minimizing What You Are Going Through]] — Reflexively shrinking the account of what is happening to you, often past the point of plausibility.
- [[behaviors/using-the-framework-to-manipulate|Using The Framework To Manipulate]] — The misuse she calls the largest online: learning someone's style in order to route around what they have said they want.
- [[behaviors/emotional-withdrawal|Emotional Withdrawal]] — The visible end of deactivation: less contact, less disclosure, less of oneself brought into the room.
- [[behaviors/exonerating-the-caregiver|Exonerating The Caregiver]] — Naming something as harm means saying your feelings were caused by someone else, which is precisely the move this system cannot make.
- [[behaviors/fixating-on-partners-flaws|Fixating On The Partner's Flaws]] — Once the threat program is running, flaws are what get logged.
- [[behaviors/insisting-you-were-right|Insisting You Were Right]] — Giving bad directions and then maintaining they were good, with the other person's navigation offered as the explanation.
- [[behaviors/using-the-framework-as-an-excuse|Using The Framework As An Excuse]] — The other misuse, and the mirror of the first: taking the diagnosis as a licence rather than a starting point.
- [[behaviors/analysing-instead-of-attuning|Analysing Instead Of Attuning]] — Priebe's own example, and an unusually candid one: an entire earlier career built on personality models, so that everyone she met could be placed on the right axes.
- [[behaviors/auditing-the-timeline|Auditing The Timeline]] — Stopping on the gaps and contradictions in a partner's account.
- [[behaviors/defensiveness|Defensiveness]] — What fight-or-flight looks like inside a conversation.
- [[behaviors/forcing-your-inner-state-to-conform|Forcing Your Inner State To Conform]] — The avoidant tell for moderate distress: growing frustrated that you cannot perform at the level you have decided is required, and treating your own depletion as a discipline problem.

## Practices

- [[practices/naming-the-feeling-accurately|Naming The Feeling Accurately]] — Say what is actually there rather than a verdict about it.
- [[practices/re-sensitizing-to-your-own-emotions|Re-Sensitizing To Your Own Emotions]] — The core of avoidant healing as she frames it: rebuilding access to the feelings that were sorted out of awareness early, rather than acquiring new coping skills on top of the numbness.
- [[practices/sensitizing-then-sharing|Sensitizing, Then Sharing]] — Her prescription for the lean-avoidant version: a daily practice of dropping attention out of the head and into the body to find what is actually there, then saying it somewhere designed to be safe —…
- [[practices/naming-the-premise-out-loud|Naming The Premise Out Loud]] — Bringing the actual premise into the open where it can be negotiated or refused.
- [[practices/taking-your-partner-at-face-value|Taking Your Partner At Face Value]] — Presenting your own position without editing and receiving theirs without decoding it.
- [[practices/re-parenting|Re-Parenting Yourself]] — Treating yourself the way a well-attuned adult would treat a child in your state: neither dismissing the feeling nor letting it run the day.
- [[practices/setting-a-boundary-you-control|Setting A Boundary You Control]] — Phrase the limit as your own action and nothing else: what you will do if a thing happens.
- [[practices/distress-tolerance|Tolerating Emotional Discomfort]] — She names this as the single capacity that most determines whether someone can sustain close relationships.
- [[practices/treating-emotion-as-data|Treating Emotion As Data]] — Take what you feel as a data point worth entering into the decision rather than a verdict to obey or a fault to correct.
- [[practices/agreeing-expectations-explicitly|Agreeing Expectations Explicitly]] — Say the expectation aloud and get an actual yes.
- [[practices/framing-it-as-a-shared-problem|Framing It As A Shared Problem]] — Bring the issue as something costing you both — time, energy, repeated arguments — and ask to work out an approach together, rather than leading with how it made you feel.
- [[practices/practising-humility|Practising Humility]] — Entering a disagreement genuinely open to being the one who is missing something.
