Avoidant people often claim other people's moods do not affect them, and Priebe's correction is precise: the moods affect them heavily and trigger flight rather than fight. Wanting out of the room is a physiologically aroused state, not a neutral one. So the avoidant is as entangled in the other person's emotion as the anxious partner — the difference is the direction of travel.
