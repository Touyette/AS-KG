An anxiously attached person is in continuous contact with their subtler feelings; a fearful-avoidant is not, and may go through a day unaware of how much is running underneath. Priebe's observation is that the emotion which does break through can therefore land harder than the anxious version of the same feeling, precisely because everything below it stayed down.
