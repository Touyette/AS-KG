Priebe's account of the fearful-avoidant cycle in the language of addiction: hooked despite knowing it is bad, then out and genuinely sober for a long stretch, then the thought that a relationship could be handled casually now — and back in. She distinguishes it from the anxious version, which craves the same drug without the adverse reaction, and notes that substance problems are common at both avoidant ends for the same reason.
