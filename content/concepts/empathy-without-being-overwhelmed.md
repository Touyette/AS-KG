Neither drowning in another person's pain nor keeping away from it in case you would. Priebe attributes it to having been contained as a child — a caregiver who saw the pain, reflected it back and was not themselves capsized by it. She notes that one common use of the word empath describes the anxious version instead: unable to tell what is theirs and what is yours, so it all has to be felt.
