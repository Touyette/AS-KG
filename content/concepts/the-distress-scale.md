Her central device here: put distress on a scale of one to ten and ask where a caregiver reliably stepped in. Infants cannot regulate themselves, so a parent effectively serves as the child's nervous system, and the level at which they intervened becomes the rulebook for what counts as distress worth attending to for the rest of that person's life.
