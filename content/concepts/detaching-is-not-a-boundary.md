Avoidant people often believe their boundaries are excellent because they are very good at detaching. Priebe separates the two: detaching is a trauma response, and a boundary is being clear about a need, saying what the limit is and why, and then living inside it. Boundaries make interdependence possible; detachment produces counter-dependence, which is a different thing wearing the same word.
