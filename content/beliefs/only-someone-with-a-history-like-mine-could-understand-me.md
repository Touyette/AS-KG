Priebe grants this one a great deal of ground. Fearful-avoidant lives often do not follow the ordinary sequence, and the coping machinery built around a complex history is genuinely hard for others to read — so finding someone who does understand is rare, and letting go of them is correspondingly hard. She calls that reasonable, and then notes what it costs when the rare person is also the wrong one.
