The conclusion a scapegoated child reaches, carried into adulthood as a standing veto on low-grade anger. Priebe describes spending most of her life certain she had an anger problem, and only later recognising that the problem ran the other way: the anger she felt at genuine injustices was never expressed, and only the overflow ever reached the surface.
