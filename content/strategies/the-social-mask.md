Most fearful-avoidant people spend more time in one response than the other, and build an identity on it — the analytical one, or the selfless and giving one — while carrying quiet embarrassment about the other. She traces the choice to whichever worked in early life, and notes the cost: the presented self is a genuine half of the person, sold as the whole.
